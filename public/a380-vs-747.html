A380 vs 747: The Two Giants Compared

The Boeing 747 and the Airbus A380 are the two largest passenger aircraft ever to enter airline service. Boeing built the 747 for more than fifty years. Airbus delivered the A380 for fourteen. This article compares the two and explains why neither is made any more.

The 747: the first wide-body

When the 747 entered service in 1970, on Pan Am's New York to London route, it was the first airliner with two aisles. It remained the largest passenger aircraft in service for 37 years.

Freight was its second career. The last version, the 747-8, was sold both as a passenger aircraft seating up to 467 in three classes and as a freighter, and the freighter sold better: by June 2021 Boeing had taken 106 orders for it against 47 for the passenger version. Boeing confirmed in 2020 that production would end, citing the market outlook after the pandemic. The last 747 built was a freighter.

The A380: a full double-decker

Airbus launched the A380 on 19 December 2000 as a €9.5 billion project. It first flew in April 2005 and entered service in October 2007. With two full passenger decks it seats 525 in a typical three-class layout, and it is certified for up to 853.

It never sold as Airbus had hoped. Development is estimated to have cost $25 billion, which the programme did not earn back. One airline, Emirates, took 123 of the 251 delivered. In February 2019 Emirates cut its outstanding order and Airbus announced that production would end. Its chief executive said that a product nobody wants, or one that sells only below the cost of making it, has to be stopped. The last A380 was delivered on 16 December 2021.

Why the twins won

Both aircraft have four engines, and that became their weakness. Until the 1980s a twin-engined airliner had to stay within an hour's flying time of an airport, which left ocean routes to aircraft with three or four engines. As engines became more reliable the limit was raised to two hours in 1985 and three in 1988, and the Boeing 777 was the first aircraft to enter service already approved for three. A twin burns less fuel than a four-engined aircraft of similar size, so once twins could cross oceans, airlines bought them.

Size was the other problem. A very large aircraft is cheap per seat only when it is full. In an all-economy layout of 853 seats, the game's A380 has the lowest fuel cost per seat of any of its flagships. But few routes sell that many seats on every departure, and an airline can instead fly two smaller twins at different times of day, which are easier to fill and give passengers a choice.

Which was the better aircraft?

By sales it was the 747, by a wide margin: 1,574 built against 254, over a production life nearly four times as long. It also found a second market in freight, which the A380 never did. By capacity it was the A380. No other airliner has been certified to carry as many people. Both went out of production for the same reason, and both are still flying.

Flying them in Tailwinds

In the game the two behave much as they did for airlines. Both are out of production, so they arrive second-hand. The 747-400 is cheap to lease and expensive to fuel, which suits a long route you are still proving. The A380 has the highest lease of any flagship and the lowest cost per seat when full, so it belongs only on the busiest trunk routes between large hubs.
