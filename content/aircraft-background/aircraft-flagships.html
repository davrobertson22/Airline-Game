The real aircraft behind these numbers

The Boeing 747 entered service with Pan Am on 22 January 1970, on the New York to London route. It was the first wide-body airliner, and it stayed the largest passenger aircraft in service until the Airbus A380 arrived in 2007. Boeing built 1,574 of them over more than half a century. The last, a 747-8 freighter, went to Atlas Air on 31 January 2023.

The A380 was launched in December 2000, first flew on 27 April 2005 and entered service with Singapore Airlines on 25 October 2007. A typical three-class cabin seats 525, and the type is certified for up to 853 passengers in an all-economy layout. Those are the two cabin sizes Tailwinds uses for it. Airbus announced in February 2019 that production would end, and delivered the last aircraft to Emirates on 16 December 2021. It was the 251st A380 delivered, and the 123rd for Emirates alone.

Both programmes ended for much the same reason. A four-engined aircraft burns more fuel than a twin of similar size, and modern twin-engined widebodies can fly the same ocean crossings. A very large aircraft is only cheap per seat when the seats are sold, and few routes fill 500 of them every day. Tailwinds treats both production lines as closed, which is why every A380 and 747 in the game arrives second-hand.

Concorde first flew on 2 March 1969 and entered service on 21 January 1976 with Air France and British Airways. Twenty were built, and fourteen of them flew for the two airlines. It cruised at about twice the speed of sound and crossed the Atlantic in just under three and a half hours, against about eight for a subsonic jet. British Airways retired its fleet on 24 October 2003, after maintenance costs rose and passenger numbers fell. Tailwinds keeps it flying.
