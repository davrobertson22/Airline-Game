The real aircraft behind these numbers

Almost every commercial freighter began as a passenger design. Some are built new as freighters, such as the 777F and the 747-8F. Many more are passenger jets converted in middle age: the conversion cuts a large cargo door into the main deck, strengthens the floor and strips out the cabin. Reported conversion prices run from about $13 million for a 767-300ER (2007) to about $40 million for a 777-200ER (2017). That is the difference between the factory freighters in the table above and the BCF, SF and P2F types: a converted jet usually leases for less, and the older ones burn more fuel for each tonne they carry.

Widebodies make natural freighters because the main deck takes two eight-foot pallets side by side. Among freighters sold to airlines, the Boeing 747-8F lifts the most at about 134 tonnes, ahead of the Airbus A350F at 111 and the Boeing 777F at 103. Tailwinds' payloads are within a few tonnes of each. Above them sit the military-derived Antonovs. The An-225, of which one was completed, could carry 250 tonnes and was the largest aircraft in the world.

The fleet is growing. The trade publication Cargo Facts forecast the world's freighter fleet rising from 1,782 aircraft in 2019 to 2,920 in 2039.
